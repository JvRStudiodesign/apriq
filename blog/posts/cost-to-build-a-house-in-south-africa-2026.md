The first question on any build is the same: **how much does it cost to build a house in South Africa in 2026?** Most answers give you a single rate per square metre. That number is where budgets go wrong, because the construction rate is only the first layer of what a house actually costs. This guide uses AprIQ's own costing engine to show the full build-up, from the base rate to the delivered figure.

## The short answer

A standard new house starts at around **R9 400 per square metre of construction**. But once you add the cost layers every real project carries — contingency, contractor profit, preliminaries, professional fees and VAT — the effective figure lands closer to **R16 000 per square metre**, before land. On a 200 m² home that is the difference between R1.88 million and roughly R3.28 million.

## What moves the number

AprIQ does not treat every house as the same house. The engine applies factors that reflect the real drivers of cost:

- **Quality of finish.** A standard specification sits at the base rate; higher standards scale it up. Choosing a high-quality finish on that same 200 m² house lifts the all-in figure from about R3.28 million to roughly R4.43 million.
- **Location.** Urban sites sit at the baseline. Suburban, peri-urban and rural sites carry progressively higher factors, because remote South African construction genuinely costs more to supply and staff.
- **Complexity.** A simple, functional design sits at the base; complex roofs, extensive glazing or specialist systems add a premium.
- **Land and site works.** Land, bulk services and slope are costed separately. A steep or unserviced stand adds materially through earthworks and servicing — costs a per-m² rate never shows.
- **Escalation.** Building in 18 months is not the same as building today. AprIQ can escalate the whole figure forward at a chosen annual rate, so the budget reflects when you actually break ground.
